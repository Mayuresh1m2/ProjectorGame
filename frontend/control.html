Playroom Quest: set up the zones

  1. Switch tracking to body pose (the button below) and start the iPhone camera, placed where it sees the stairs and the wall in full.
  2. Press Use live camera frame as photo (or upload a photo taken from the phone's position).
  3. Press Add zone, click the corners of the stairs (or the climbing wall) on the photo, then Finish shape. Choose what triggers it and what it rewards.
  4. Save zones, open the game from the menu (🏆 Playroom Quest), and test with the buttons on the right. Walk, jump and climb in front of the camera; the skeleton should follow you here.

Projector's own browser no cable

  1. Put the projector (or the TV box on it) on the same Wi-Fi as this laptop.
  2. In its browser, scan this QR code or type the link. Accept the certificate warning once.
  3. It opens the game full screen and stays paired (a cookie in that browser). To lock out a display, press Revoke displays.

A paired display only receives what the game draws from (hand positions, the scene, settings): never the camera picture, and it cannot change anything. If its browser is slow or will not run the game, use ▶ Open game on this laptop over HDMI instead. Hand positions travel over your Wi-Fi to the display.

3 · What the laptop sees

Waiting for camera…

Camera → wall calibration

  1. Open the game window on the projector. A green grid appears on it while this tab is open. Use ＋ Add grid for a second wall or section: each grid gets its own calibration and hands are mapped through the grid they are in.
  2. Move and resize the grid in Projector grid (drag its corners or inside it) so all four corners are on the wall and inside the camera's view. The grid on the wall follows live.
  3. Press Auto-find corners: the projector flashes four dots and the camera locates them. Or drag the four numbered handles on the camera view onto the grid's corners yourself.
  4. It saves as soon as the four handles are placed. Wave a hand: the green dot in Play area (test) should follow it. Set mirror / rotation first so the camera view looks like the wall.

Changing mirror/rotation clears the saved calibration (its points belonged to the old orientation). Moving the grid just clears the handles. The hand should be near the wall: the mapping is exact for the wall plane, and a hand held closer to the camera appears shifted (parallax). Auto-find works best with the room lights dimmed and the phone not moving.

    Resolution & aspect

    Projector notes

    • Connect the projector over HDMI and set Settings → Displays → Join / Extend (not Mirror), so the control panel stays on the laptop.
    • Use Toggle test grid to aim and zoom the projector. The border should fit the area you want to play on.
    • If the projector's own digital keystone is on, calibrate after setting it, and recalibrate if it changes: the camera sees the final image on the wall, so calibration absorbs it, but the picture is resampled and gets softer.
    • One projector can only light surfaces it can physically see: a floor, a door and a wall rarely fit in one beam. Use the Layout tab to give each visible region its own content.

    Layout editor

    Drag a frame to move it, drag the corner handles to resize (untick “keep rectangle” to warp onto a slanted surface), drag the round handle to rotate. Changes appear on the output immediately.

    Frames & surfaces

    Put video files (MP4/H.264 or WebM) and pictures (PNG, JPG, WebP, GIF, SVG) in the media/ folder of the project. They stay on this laptop.

    Hand tracking

    Smoothing uses a One-Euro filter: lower min cutoff = steadier but laggier; higher speed gain = less lag during fast moves.

    Testing without the camera

    The game also responds to mouse and touch on the output window.
